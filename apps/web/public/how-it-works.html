How ScanIn works

Attendance that is quick for an honest student and awkward for a proxy. Here is the whole flow.

1. The lecturer starts a session

A lecturer opens a class session for one of their courses and projects a QR code. The code is a short-lived signed token that refreshes every 30 seconds, so a photo of it is useless within moments. The lecturer can close check-in or cancel the code on screen whenever they like.

2. Students scan on their phones

Each student signs in to their own account and scans the code. A check-in is tied to the student and their device, and the database allows exactly one effective attendance record per student per session. One phone cannot check two students into the same class.

3. The record is sealed

When check-in closes, the session's attendance is fixed. Later corrections are possible but are always recorded as revisions with a reason, so history is never silently rewritten.

4. Anti-proxy controls, as signals

ScanIn compares a quick room headcount with the number checked in, can draw a randomised sample of students for an in-person spot check, and keeps a review trail. These are signals for the lecturer to act on, not automatic penalties.

5. Optional face verification

For courses that turn it on, a student who has consented and enrolled can complete a quick liveness-checked face match after checking in. It is a review signal only; it never changes an attendance record, and no raw image is ever stored, only an encrypted template reference. A failed or unavailable check simply routes the student to an in-person check.

6. History and exports

Every session rolls up per course, for both lecturers and students, and can be exported to CSV, Excel, PDF or Word.

No precise location is collected, and nothing is written to official university systems. Read our privacy notice.
